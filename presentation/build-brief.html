Book container trucks like a cab, and reuse the empty container down the road.

We're building a booking platform for container transport in India, and the idea comes from the JNPT, Pune and Aurangabad corridor. Exporters book, fleet owners supply the trucks, and when an import container is emptied near an exporter who needs one, we reuse it for that export with the shipping line's permission. This page walks through the idea, what we've decided so far, and what we still need to settle tonight.

Today the same kind of container travels empty twice.

Take one corridor, JNPT to Waluj. An import container comes up full, gets unloaded at the factory, and the shipping line expects it back at its depot near the port. A few kilometres away, an exporter needs the same size of container from the same line, so a different empty container makes the trip up from that same depot.

That's two long runs with nothing paid inside the box, for containers that could have been one. Switch to "With reuse" to see what changes.

Five kinds of people, one backend.

Exporters book. Fleet owners bring the trucks and drivers. Admin steps in when something breaks. Shipping lines say yes or no to reusing their containers. All of it runs on one Convex backend, so when a driver taps "Loaded", the exporter and admin see it at the same moment, without anyone refreshing.

One booking can carry many containers, and each one gets its own life.

Exporters often ship more than one container at a time. So the exporter books once and pays once, and the platform turns every container into its own job, with its own truck, driver and status. If three find trucks and the fourth doesn't, the three still go. Admin can assign the fourth by hand before the cut-off, and if that fails, only the fourth is refunded.

For now we only take standard dry containers, one per truck.

Payment

We price with a formula, not a rate card.

A rate card for every lane in India is thousands of rows that nobody keeps up to date. A formula needs about 15 numbers, and diesel prices come in by state on their own. There's a nice side effect: when we reuse a container nearby, the empty distance drops, so the price drops too, without any special reuse logic.

These numbers are placeholders so we can watch the formula move. Real mileage, cost per km, minimum fares and our fee come from a few fleet owners. That's open question 1.

        

      

      

        
rate
What one kilometre costs the truck: diesel price in that state divided by the truck's mileage, plus a fixed cost per km for that container size.

        
km_empty × r
The customer pays a share r of the empty driving. This is the term reuse shrinks.

        
min_fare
A floor for very short trips, so a 5 km job still makes sense for the truck.

        
waiting
Hours at the factory beyond the free hours, at an hourly rate. Billed after the trip as a second payment.

        
demand
Open jobs divided by available trucks in the area, capped so it can't run away. Something like 1.0 to 1.5.

      

    


    
    

      
We match the container before it's even empty.

      
This is the part that makes reuse work in practice. An import booking already tells us the container number, shipping line, size, factory and when it arrives. So the moment the import is booked, we mark that container "empty soon", and matching can find an export for it while it's still on the highway. By the time it's unloaded, the next job is waiting, and the same truck can drive straight there.

How we pick the match

          
    
            
  • Hard filters first: same shipping line, same size, and time windows that overlap.
    • 
            
  • Then rank by the shortest distance from importer to exporter, and after that the container closest to its free-time deadline, so nobody pays detention.
    • 
            
  • Prefer the truck that delivered the import. Then we don't need a second truck at all.
    • 
          

        

        

          
What keeps it safe

          
    
            
  • Reserving a container is atomic. One container can never be promised to two exporters.
    • 
            
  • A match is never an approval. The shipping line decides.
    • 
            
  • Fleet owners can still list empties from imports done off the platform, by hand.
    • 
          

        

      

    


    
    

      
The exporter pays upfront, and the transporter gets paid at the port.

      
The exporter pays when they confirm the booking. We hold that money until the container gates in at the port, then give the exporter 24 hours to raise a problem. If nothing comes up, it releases on its own. Admin only deals with the trips that actually went wrong.

Which payment product

          
We wanted Razorpay to hold the money for us. Here's where that stands.

          

            

              
OptionWhere it stands

              
                
Razorpay RouteNot at launch. It now needs more than ₹40 lakh turnover, and Razorpay switched it off for smaller businesses from 1 Jan 2026.

Invoices and tax

          
    
            
  • The freight invoice is in the transporter's name. The platform generates it for them.
    • 
            
  • The exporter usually pays GST on freight under reverse charge. The transporter can choose to charge it themselves.
    • 
            
  • We invoice only our own fee. The client's CA confirms all of this.
    • 
            
  • We build the money flow so the payment product underneath can change later.
    • 
          

        

      

    


    
    

      
When something goes wrong, whoever caused it pays.

      
The rule is simple: whoever caused the problem, or is most responsible for it, carries the cost. Cancellation fees go to the side that lost the job, never to us.

      

        

          
What happenedWhat follows

          
            
Cancelled before a transporter acceptsFree.

            
Exporter cancels after acceptanceA fixed fee goes to the transporter, taken from the exporter's refund.

            
Exporter cancels after the truck arrivesThe fee plus waiting.

            
Fleet owner cancels after acceptingA charge on their next booking, and they drop in matching rank for a while. Repeat cancellations go to admin.

            
Port cut-off missed, truck lateThe fleet owner pays a penalty, charged on their next booking.

            
Port cut-off missed, factory delayThe exporter pays waiting.

            
Port cut-off missed, port congestionNobody pays.

          
        

      

      
The port cut-off isn't a hard filter. Matching ranks trucks that can make it higher. If the best truck's ETA is already past the cut-off, the exporter sees that before confirming, and during the trip the live ETA alerts the exporter and admin when it's at risk.

    


    
    

      
Built for cheap Android phones and patchy highway signal.

      
Drivers are the people we can least afford to slow down, so the driver app gets a performance budget we measure, not a feeling. And the maps have to stay smooth with hundreds of trucks on them, which is where sb struggled.

      

        

          
Driver app

          
    
            
  • React Native with Expo, the New Architecture and Hermes.
    • 
            
  • Offline first. Status and photos save on the phone and sync later, so the driver never waits on a spinner.
    • 
            
  • Photos compressed on the phone. Location recorded only during an active trip.
    •
