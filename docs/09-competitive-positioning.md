## Proposed position

Become the preferred container transport service for repeat exporters in one industrial cluster. Offer a confirmed transport plan, clear charges and someone responsible for getting the job completed.

The registration and refund issues found in research are useful product improvements, but competitors can copy them. A lasting advantage must come from acquiring customers, fulfilling their bookings and making money on that work.

## 1. Choose the first market around relationships we can secure

MatchLog already has shipping-line relationships, and its MiSTY network includes Pune. Expect competition there. [Hapag-Lloyd directory](https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/en/services-information/procurement-supplier/container-applications.html), [MiSTY locations](https://matchlog.delivery/misty/)

Start with the smallest group where we can secure recurring export demand, visibility into incoming containers and dependable transporters.

The shipping line, container type and availability window must align. Ten regular customers with compatible movements could be more useful than hundreds of scattered registrations. This is a strategic hypothesis, not a validated volume target.

## 2. Sell the complete transport job

The buyer hypothesis is that an exporter cares most about having a suitable container at the factory when loading starts, then reaching the port within the agreed window.

Handle approved reuse and ordinary depot sourcing. This keeps the service useful when reuse is unavailable. Reuse improves the economics of eligible bookings.

Proposed wording for early customer conversations:

> Share your loading plan. We arrange the container and transport, confirm the price before payment, and coordinate the movement through port gate-in.

This describes the intended offer, not current software capability or a financial guarantee. MatchLog's TES overlaps with the offer. Better execution for the chosen customers must establish the difference. [TES](https://matchlog.delivery/tes/)

## 3. Win a recurring share of the customer's business

Offer to handle a small, recurring allocation of an exporter's movements. Let performance determine whether that allocation grows. Replacing an established arrangement at once would create a larger sales obstacle.

The initial buyer should have repeat shipments, enough coordination work to feel the problem and authority to try another provider.

Customs agents and forwarders could help acquire customers. Give partners visibility, agreed compensation and clear rules about customer ownership. They need a commercial reason to introduce us.

Compare completed jobs using final cost, pickup reliability, gate-in timing and how much chasing the customer had to do.

## 4. Arrange supply ahead of the booking

Ask participating exporters for upcoming loading plans and import-side partners for expected unloading times. Arrange compatible containers and transporter capacity ahead of the request where possible.

The operating advantage would come from knowing which containers are likely to become available, which approvals are feasible and which fleet owners honour commitments.

Import-side partners must benefit too, through an agreed reduction in return coordination or costs. Keeping availability current cannot depend on indefinite unpaid work from partners.

Accurate availability records and dependable relationships could make offers more reliable. Measure that result. Data collection alone is not a demonstrated advantage.

## 5. Make the economics work without permanent discounts

Savings must benefit the customer, keep the transporter interested and pay for the platform's operation.

Test the proposed booking fee against collection costs and manual support. Illustratively, collecting INR 30,000, earning a fee of INR 750 and paying a 2% collection cost leaves INR 150 before support, acquisition or failures. These are assumptions, not provider quotes or agreed prices.

Measure contribution per completed booking after all variable costs. Track cash exposure separately. If a carrier requires payment before approval, the proposed customer payment policy may require the platform to advance that money or negotiate another arrangement.

Provide an accountable operations contact. Define financial guarantees only after measuring failure rates and recovery costs and settling liability with the team.

## 6. Expand after repeat bookings are sustainable

Run the first commercial test with a small group of recurring exporters and compatible supply partners. Evaluate repeat paid bookings, dependable completion and positive contribution after operational costs.

Test difficult bookings too. A rejected container, delayed truck or cancellation must get resolved without the customer coordinating everyone themselves.

Expand to nearby customers whose movements strengthen the existing supply network once the initial operation works consistently.

## Evidence needed when this discussion resumes

Establish which exporters will allocate recurring shipments, which import partners will share timely availability and which shipping lines will support the approval process. These commitments determine whether the proposed position is achievable and where to launch.

The recommendations above are unvalidated commercial hypotheses. Public research supports the cited competitor capabilities; it does not establish our advantage, customer willingness to switch, obtainable margins or operational performance.
